# Domain Coverage and Expansion

Maintain coverage across all academically valuable fields, including:

- official statistics and public administration;
- earth, climate, ocean, atmosphere, geospatial, and space science;
- biology, ecology, genomics, medicine, clinical research, and public health;
- physics, astronomy, chemistry, and materials science;
- engineering, manufacturing, infrastructure, energy, and sensors;
- computing, software, AI benchmarks, networks, and cybersecurity;
- agriculture, food, forestry, fisheries, water, and biodiversity;
- economics, finance, business, trade, labor, and development;
- sociology, psychology, demography, education, politics, law, and communication;
- humanities, history, archaeology, language, arts, media, and cultural heritage;
- publications, citations, researchers, grants, patents, standards, and innovation;
- general repositories, restricted collections, and commercial databases.

Audit each domain for:

- primary and harmonized sources;
- aggregate and microdata;
- cross-sectional and longitudinal coverage;
- spatial, temporal, network, sequence, sensor, image, document, and multimodal data;
- objective and subjective measurements;
- experimental, observational, administrative, modeled, and forecast data;
- global, regional, national, subnational, local, and non-geographic coverage;
- open, application-based, secure, physical, subscription, and commercial access;
- non-English and historically marginalized sources.

Expand continuously through official institutional catalogs, upstream provenance, repository networks, dataset citations, data papers, standards, expert nominations, and warehouse coverage failures. Use numerical targets only for workload planning, never as completion criteria.
